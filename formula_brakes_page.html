Northwestern Formula Racing

Formula Racing Brakes

Brake Rotor Lead | 2023 – 2024 Season

Context

As part of Northwestern’s Formula SAE team, I led the design and implementation of the brake rotor system for the 2024 combustion car. I was responsible for taking the rotor from first principles calculations through CAD, analysis, manufacturing, and validation on the finished car.

Goal

Lead the design of a lightweight, thermally robust brake rotor that meets Formula SAE stopping distance rules while surviving repeated track sessions, and integrate it into the 2024 car.

Steps Taken

Analysis & Concept Development

  • Calculated rotor dimensions, torque requirements, and clamp forces in MATLAB using vehicle mass, tire data, and FSAE rules.
  • Benchmarked prior team rotors and professional motorsport designs to set mass and temperature targets.
  • Sketched alternative slot/vent patterns to balance cooling, pad cleaning, and manufacturability.

Detail Design & Validation

  • Modeled the full rotor in Solidworks and ran structural FEA to verify factor of safety at peak braking torque.
  • Performed transient thermal analysis in ANSYS to predict temperature rise over repeated stops.
  • Designed floating hardware and pins to accommodate thermal expansion without warping the hat.

Manufacturing & Documentation

  • Manufactured rotors in-house using laser cutters, manual mills, surface grinders, and lathes.
  • Specified critical tolerances and runout and produced GD&T drawings for outsourced spare rotors.
  • Worked with the team’s suspension and powertrain leads to confirm wheel package and caliper clearances.

Testing & Iteration

  • Measured rotor temperatures and pad wear during skidpad and endurance practice to validate simulations.
  • Adjusted slot pattern and vent geometry to improve cooling without increasing NVH or cracking risk.
  • Documented a full rotor design guide for future Northwestern Formula teams.

Results

Successful On-Car Performance

Final rotor design was installed on the 2024 car and completed dynamic events without cracking, warping, or fade. Rotor temperatures and stopping distances met the team’s performance targets.

Design Adopted for Future Cars

My unique rotor profile and float hardware were carried forward to subsequent Northwestern Formula cars, becoming the baseline for future iterations of the braking system.

By owning the process from analysis to manufacturing, I delivered a brake package that was hall-effect compatible, maintained structural safety margins, and gave the team a repeatable design workflow for future seasons.
